# Sabil — Business Plan (draft v1, 2026-11-09)

Status: **draft for discussion.** Numbers marked *TBD* need the owner's real

---

## 1. Summary

Sabil is a free community map of public drinking-water coolers in Egypt. People
find nearby coolers, see if they work, and confirm or report them.

**The insight:** users will never pay (they need *free* water), and small-shop
advertising does not work at our size. The people who *already spend money on
coolers* are families and organisations who build a **sabil as sadaqa jariya**,
often in the name of a deceased relative. Their problem: after paying, they have
no idea whether the cooler still works, and nobody maintains it.

**Sabil's role:** the trust layer that proves a charity cooler is alive, through
daily crowd confirmations, photos and a "people served" count. We combine that
proof with a **build-and-maintain service** that people pay for.

**Model:** free app for users; revenue from (A) building sabils for donors,
(B) maintenance subscriptions, later (C) corporate CSR networks.

---

## 2. Problem

| Who | Problem today |
|---|---|
| People on the street (workers, riders, students) | Don't know where free water is, or whether a cooler works / has cups |
| Families who fund a sabil | Pay once, then no proof it works; filters not changed; cooler breaks or runs empty |
| Mosques, shops, buildings that own coolers | Maintenance is ad hoc; they hear about breakdowns late |
| Companies doing CSR | Hard to show measurable, local, visible impact |

---

## 3. Solution

1. **The app (free):** map, status, confirmations, photos, Arabic + English.
2. **"Build a sabil" service:** we install a cooler in a chosen location,
   dedicated in a name, and maintain it.
3. **Patron page:** each funded cooler shows its dedication and live proof
   (status history, confirmations, photos, people served). Shareable link for the
   family.
4. **Maintenance plans:** crowd reports trigger repairs; owners subscribe.

---

## 4. Customers and what they pay for

| Segment | Pays for | Why they buy |
|---|---|---|
| Families / individuals (incl. diaspora) | Sabil package (install + 1st year maintenance), then yearly renewal | Ongoing charity with proof; dedication to a loved one |
| Mosques, shops, buildings with existing coolers | Monthly/yearly maintenance plan | Their cooler keeps working without effort; good reputation |
| Companies (water brands, banks, telcos, developers) | Branded cooler networks + impact reports | Visible CSR, especially summer and Ramadan |
| NGOs | Partnership (they collect donations, we install/maintain/prove) | Transparency for their donors |

**Users never pay.**

## 5. Revenue options

### A. Build a sabil (core, start here)
- One-time package: cooler + installation + plaque + 12 months maintenance +
  patron page. Price = costs + margin. *TBD*
- Renewal: yearly maintenance after year one. *TBD*
- Peaks: summer, Ramadan, and after a death in the family (ongoing demand).

### B. Maintenance subscriptions
- For existing coolers we did not install.
- Delivered by partner technicians; we take a commission or margin.
- Leads come from the app: "reported broken by 3 people this week".

### C. Corporate CSR networks (after traction)
- Fund and brand N coolers in a city; branded cups and plaque; in-app patron card;
  quarterly impact report from app data.
- Needs proof first: number of coolers, monthly confirmations, cities.

### D. Refill / repair donations (add-on, legal check needed)
- People fund filters, cups or repairs for a specific cooler; we take a fee.
- Public fundraising in Egypt is regulated (NGO law); likely run through a
  registered NGO partner.

### Not doing
- Charging users; heavy ads; data sales to government (maybe much later).

## 8. Go-to-market: validate before building

**Weeks 1–2:** get quotes for 1 cooler installation and a year of maintenance;
set the package price; find 1–2 technician partners.

**Weeks 2–6:** manual sales test, no app changes:
- Post the offer in local WhatsApp/Facebook groups and mosque communities, and
  share with personal networks and diaspora contacts.
- Message: build a sabil in your loved one's name, maintained by us, with live
  proof in the app.
- Take payment manually (InstaPay / bank transfer) with a simple written agreement.

**Success target:** 5–10 paid sabils. If met → build in-app "Build a sabil" +
patron pages + payments (Paymob/Fawry). If not → change price or message and retest.

**In parallel:** keep growing the map in one area (start: Maadi) so patron pages
show real confirmations.

## 9. Product changes this plan implies (later)

- Rename "sponsor" to **patron**: dedication text, story, photo, impact counter
  ("confirmed working 312 times", "last checked 2 h ago").
- Shareable patron page (link opens in the app or the browser).
- "Build a sabil" request form in the app → lead in the admin panel.
- Admin: list of patron coolers, maintenance schedule, alerts when a patron
  cooler is reported broken or not confirmed for N days.
- Monthly report to patrons (WhatsApp/email): status, confirmations, photos.

---

## 10. Metrics to track

- Coolers on the map; % confirmed in the last 7 days.
- Weekly active contributors; confirmations per week.
- Paid sabils; renewals; maintenance subscribers.
- Time from "broken" report to fixed (patron coolers).
- Cost and margin per sabil.

---

## 11. Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
|---|---|
| Fundraising rules (NGO law) | Sell a *service* (install + maintain) as a company; donations only via a registered NGO partner; get legal advice |
| Maintenance quality | Vetted technician partners, photo proof per visit, crowd reports as quality check |
| Too few users to show proof | Focus on one area first; seed coolers; ask patrons' families to confirm |
| Vandalism or theft of coolers | Placement with a responsible host (shop or mosque); repairs reserve |
| Fake confirmations | 50 m radius + GPS accuracy check; trust scores; admin review |
