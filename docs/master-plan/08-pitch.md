# Nwasy: the Shark Tank Egypt pitch

Role: pitch coach for TV investor shows.
Date: 2026-11-11.

**Read this first.** Every number in [brackets] is a target from the business
plan, not a result. The pitch must not be given until those brackets hold real
numbers. A pitch today would be "an app, no users, no sales", and the answer
would be no.

## When to apply

The show takes applications all year through its official site [1]. Apply
when stage 4 of the business plan is passed:

| Must be true | Target |
|---|---|
| Places on the map | [1,000 coolers] |
| Monthly users | [10,000] |
| Paid sabils | [100] |
| Paying vendors | [60] |
| Districts open | [3] |
| Company registered, name and trademark filed | Yes |

## The ask

**EGP 4 million for 15% of the company** (a value of about EGP 26.7 million
after the investment).

How that compares with deals reported from the show: 3 million for 10% for a
medical-device startup [3]; 3.75 million for 15% plus a loan for a shopping
app [3]; 12.5 million for 27% for a health company [3]. Sharks often counter
with a larger share. Decide the floor before walking in: **no more than 25%
for the 4 million**, and say no beyond that.

This ask assumes the targets above are met, including roughly EGP 3 to 4
million of sales in the past twelve months. With less, ask for less.

**What the money does**

| Use | EGP |
|---|---|
| Open 20 districts at about 20,000 each | 400,000 |
| Bulk stock of coolers (lower unit cost) | 900,000 |
| Team for 12 months: operations, sales, a developer | 1,700,000 |
| Product: phone login, notifications, vendor tools | 500,000 |
| Marketing and seasonal campaigns | 500,000 |

## The 90-second open

Bring a real cooler plaque with a QR code, and a phone.

> "في عزّ الصيف، في نص الشارع، عايز تشرب. فين أقرب كولدير؟ وشغّال ولا لأ؟
> محدّش يعرف."
> (Midsummer, middle of the street, you need water. Where is the nearest
> cooler, and is it working? Nobody knows.)
>
> "I'm [name], and this is Nwasy, نواصي: the map of everything on the street
> corner. We started with free drinking water. Today [1,000] coolers in
> [three] districts are on our map, and [10,000] people use it every month."
>
> "Here is what makes it trustworthy: you can only confirm a cooler works if
> your phone is within 50 metres of it. So what the map says is there, is
> there."
>
> "And here is who pays. Families build these coolers as sadaqa jariya for
> someone they lost. They pay once and never know if it still works. We build
> it, look after it, and give them this." (Scan the plaque. Show the page.)
> "[312] strangers confirmed this one. The family sees that every day.
> [100] families have paid us about 25,000 pounds each."
>
> "Now the same map shows food trucks and coffee carts, which move every day
> and are on no map. [60] of them pay us monthly to be found."
>
> "We are asking for 4 million pounds for 15% to open twenty more districts."

## Seven slides, if slides are allowed

1. The corner: one photo, one question ("where is the nearest working cooler?").
2. The product: the map, the 50 m rule, the confirmation.
3. Who pays: the family, the plaque, the proof page.
4. Second layer: vendors that move, "open here".
5. Numbers: places, users, sales, renewals, vendors.
6. The district playbook: 20,000 pounds, eight weeks, paid back by four sales.
7. The ask and what it buys.

## The hard questions

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| "This is a charity, not a business." | "Users never pay. Families pay us for a service: build, maintain, prove. We keep [23%] on a sabil and [37%] on a care plan, and vendor plans are software at about 80%." |
| "Your margins are thin." | "On water, yes, and we say so. Water buys the audience and the trust. The software income from vendors grows on top: it is under a fifth of revenue in our plan and over 40% of profit by year three." |
| "Why won't Google Maps do this?" | "Google maps addresses. A cooler has none, and a cart moves. Our data comes from people standing at the place, and from the vendor checking in each morning." |
| "What stops a charity or a big app copying you?" | "They can copy the app. They cannot copy [1,000] verified places, the plaques and stickers already on the street, or the hosts and vendors we know by name. We would rather partner with the charities; they need the proof we produce." |
| "Is collecting this money legal?" | "We do not collect donations. A customer buys installation and maintenance from a company. Our lawyer confirmed [what was confirmed]. Shared sabils go through a registered charity partner." |
| "What if someone gets sick from a cooler?" | "Each cooler has a filter log on its page, a host agreement, and a report button that reaches us at once. [Insurance arrangement.]" |
| "People will fake confirmations for points." | "You must be within 50 metres with accurate GPS, points are capped per day, repeated attempts are blocked, and reports from new users wait for review. We tested these attacks on our own system." |
| "Will vendors really pay?" | "[60] already do, at [250] pounds a month, in districts where we have users. We do not charge where we have no audience yet." |
| "Why you?" | Your own answer: why this street, why this problem, what you did by hand before any investor. |
| "What is the valuation based on?" | "About [seven] times last year's sales, with [70%] of customers renewing and a new district paying for itself in four sales." |
| "What happens to the 4 million if districts fail?" | "A district costs 20,000 to try and has a pass/fail number at week eight. We stop a failing district, not the company." |

## Numbers to know by heart

Cost to build a sabil. Price. Profit on one. Care plan price and profit.
Renewal rate. Cost to win a customer. Cost to open a district. Monthly users.
Share of coolers confirmed in the last week. Paying vendors and their monthly
price. Cash in the bank and months it lasts.

If you cannot say one of these without looking, you are not ready.

## What would sink the pitch

- Saying "super app" before showing one thing that works.
- Any number you cannot explain.
- "There is no competition." Say who is near and why you differ.
- Using religious language to sell on television.
- No paying customers.
