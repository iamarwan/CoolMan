# Nwasy business plan: from zero to the Shark Tank pitch

Roles: founder-operator, CFO, startup lawyer's checklist.
Version 2, 2026-11-11. Replaces `docs/business-plan.md`.

**How to read the numbers.** A figure with a source number like [3] comes from
the list at the end. A figure marked *assumption* is my estimate and must be
replaced by a real quote or real sales. Nothing here is legal advice; section 9
says what a lawyer must confirm.

---

## 1. The business in one page

**What it is.** Nwasy (نواصي, "street corners") is a map of the useful things
on every street corner. It starts with free public drinking-water coolers and
grows into food trucks, street coffee stands and other things people look for
on foot. The app runs today in English and Arabic with the water layer built.

**Why water first.** It is free, needed every day in the heat, and nobody maps
it. It brings people in without advertising, and it builds the one thing every
later layer depends on: a crowd that confirms, on the spot, that a place is
really there and really working.

**The engine: "be there" proof.** A confirmation only counts when the phone is
within 50 metres of the place. That rule is built and tested. It is what makes
a status trustworthy, and it works the same for "this cooler has cold water"
and "this coffee cart is open on this corner right now".

**Who pays.**

| Payer | What they buy | When |
|---|---|---|
| Families and organisations who fund public coolers as *sadaqa jariya* (ongoing charity) | **Build a sabil:** a cooler installed in a chosen spot with a dedication, a year of upkeep, and a live page proving it works | From month 1. Needs no audience, so it funds the early months. |
| Owners of existing coolers (mosques, shops, buildings) | **Care plan:** a yearly upkeep plan | From month 6 |
| Street vendors (food trucks, coffee and tea carts) | **Vendor plan:** a highlighted pin, menu and photos, and "open now" alerts to followers. Basic listing stays free. | Only in districts where the app already has users, from about month 9 |
| Companies | **Networks:** many coolers under their name, with impact reports | Year 2 |

Users never pay.

**Where it stands.** Working app, admin panel and server, tested on one phone.
No company, no users, no revenue. Nothing has been sold yet.

**The plan in one line.** Prove one district (Maadi): map it, sell the first
sabils by hand, launch publicly, add vendors once people use the map, then
repeat district by district and apply to Shark Tank Egypt with real numbers.

## 2. Problem and evidence

| Who | Problem | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| People working and walking outside | Heat and no reliable free water. Nobody knows where a cooler is or whether it works. | Summer 2026 heat reached about 45°C in parts of Egypt and disrupted work [14]. Estimates of delivery riders alone run from about 2 million [12] to 6 million [13]; the sources disagree. |
| Families who fund a cooler | They pay once, then have no proof it works and nobody maintains it. | Newspapers publish cooler price guides "for sadaqa jariya" [5], which shows steady demand. *Assumption to test:* they will pay more for upkeep and proof. |
| Street vendors | A cart or truck moves, has no address, and regular maps do not show whether it is open today or where. | Food trucks grew strongly after 2020 [23]. Tea and coffee cars along roads are a visible, mostly unregulated trend [24]. Licensing exists under Law 92/2018 [25]. No search result showed an app that locates them. *Assumption to test:* vendors will pay for being found. |
| Companies | They want visible, measurable good works, especially in summer and Ramadan. | Delivery platforms run summer care campaigns for riders [15]. |

**What is not known yet:** how many coolers and street vendors a district has,
how many coolers are broken on a given day, what families pay installers
today, and what a vendor would pay per month. Stage 0 answers these on foot.

## 3. The road: six stages with pass/fail gates

Do not start a stage until the gate before it is passed. If a gate fails
twice, rethink that stage, not the next one.

| Stage | When | What happens | Gate to pass |
|---|---|---|---|
| **0. Foundations** | Weeks 1 to 2 | Quotes from 3 cooler suppliers and 2 technicians. Walk Maadi and map every cooler (target 150); note every food truck and coffee cart seen. One paid hour with a lawyer. Check the name. | Written price list with real costs. 150 coolers mapped. A lawyer's yes on selling "build and care" as a service. Name cleared (section 9). |
| **1. Prove someone pays** | Weeks 3 to 8 | Sell sabils by hand: WhatsApp, Facebook groups, mosque communities, own network. Bank transfer or InstaPay with a one-page agreement. Install the first coolers. In parallel, ask 20 vendors what being found is worth to them (no selling yet). | **5 paid sabils** leaving at least 20% after all costs. 20 vendor conversations written up. If fewer than 5 sales after 40 serious conversations, change the price or the offer. |
| **2. Public launch in one district** | Months 3 to 5 | Company registered. App in the Play Store. Patron pages. Content running (`03-content-plan.md`). Free listings for vendors open (`05-super-app-roadmap.md`, layer 2). | 300 coolers mapped, 1,000 monthly users, 60% of coolers confirmed in the last 7 days, 20 paid sabils in total, 50 vendors listed for free. |
| **3. Make sales repeatable** | Months 6 to 9 | Care plans sold to cooler owners using broken-cooler reports. First ops hire. First paid vendor plans in the launch district. First company pilot (10 coolers). | 50 paid sabils, 30 care plans, **20 paying vendors**, one company pilot, a new customer acquired for under 15% of the sale price. |
| **4. Expand** | Months 9 to 12 | Two more districts with the checklist in `04-expansion-plan.md`. Apply to the show. | 1,000 coolers mapped, 10,000 monthly users, 100 paid sabils, 60 paying vendors, 70% of first-year customers say they will renew. |
| **5. Pitch** | When stage 4's gate is met | `08-pitch.md`. | Application accepted. |

The show takes applications all year [1]. Apply at the stage 4 gate, not
before: a pitch without paying customers gets "come back when you have sales".

## 4. Market size, built from the bottom

There is no reliable official figure for charitable giving in Egypt; published
numbers range from a 2017 government study of about EGP 4.5 billion a year to
far larger unofficial ones [16]. Food-service market reports exist but are
commissioned forecasts [23]. I use neither.

Count what Nwasy can actually serve:

| Layer | How it is counted | Size |
|---|---|---|
| Internet users in Egypt | DataReportal | about 98 to 99 million [10] |
| First district (Maadi) | *Assumption:* 150 to 300 public coolers, 50 to 150 street vendors | Measured in stage 0 |
| Greater Cairo | *Assumption:* 20,000 to 40,000 coolers | Estimated after stage 0 from coolers per km² |
| Year 3 targets | Model | 900 sabils sold in the year, about 1,800 coolers under care, about 2,100 paying vendors |

The honest line for investors: "Nobody knows the national number, which is the
point of the map. In Maadi we counted X coolers and Y vendors in Z km²; W% of
coolers were not working; N families and M vendors paid us in the first months."

## 5. Competition

| Who | What they do | How we differ |
|---|---|---|
| Large charities (for example Orman's piped-water connections at EGP 6,800 each, payable in shares [7]) | Collect donations for water projects, mostly in villages. Report by news and photos. | We sell a service with continuous proof for one cooler the donor can visit. They are also possible partners (section 9). |
| Donation apps (MegaKheir lists a water category and over 170 charities [8]) | Payment channel to charities. | Same as above. A possible sales channel later. |
| Cooler shops and installers | Sell and install the unit. | No upkeep, no proof. They are our suppliers. |
| Google Maps | Fixed businesses with addresses. | Weak on things without an address: coolers, carts that move, "is it open right now on this corner". |
| Delivery and menu apps (talabat, elmenus [23]) | Restaurants with kitchens and delivery. | Street vendors mostly are not on them; we do not deliver, we help people find what is nearby on foot. |
| Water-finder apps abroad (Tap planned to earn from sponsored refills [17]; "Water Finder" lists fountains worldwide [18]) | Maps of refill points. | None found for Egyptian coolers, and advert money did not make them large. Our money comes from donors and vendors. |
| Rahaa | Map of public toilets in Egypt and the Arab world [19]. | Shows the idea has takers; a possible partner for a later layer. |

Searches on 2026-11-11 showed no app mapping Egypt's public coolers or its
food trucks and carts. That is not proof none exists; check the Play Store
before the pitch.

## 6. What we sell and what it costs

All figures in Egyptian pounds. Costs marked *assumption* come from retail
price guides, not supplier quotes.

### Building one sabil

| Item | Cost | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| 3-tap stainless street cooler | 9,000 | Retail guides show 5,500 to 10,000 and more for premium units [5][6] |
| Filter housing and first cartridges | 1,000 | *Assumption* |
| Installation | 2,000 | *Assumption* |
| Plaque with QR code | 500 | *Assumption* |
| **One-time cost** | **12,500** | |
| Cartridges for a year | 1,800 | Sets at 450 to 500, three to four changes [20] |
| 12 technician visits at 250 | 3,000 | *Assumption* |
| Repairs reserve | 1,200 | *Assumption* |
| **Care cost per year** | **6,000** | |

### Prices and profit per sale

| Product | Price | Direct cost | Profit | Margin |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Build a sabil (cooler, install, plaque, first year of care, proof page) | 24,900 | 18,500 + 2.5% payment fees | 5,778 | 23% |
| Care plan, one year | 9,900 | 6,000 + fees | 3,652 | 37% |
| Company network, per cooler | 22,000 | 18,500 + fees | 2,950 | 13% |
| Vendor plan, per month | 250 | 20% (fees, support, sales commission) | 200 | 80% |

All four prices are *assumptions* until stage 1. Not in the sabil price and
agreed per site: **electricity and water** (paid by the host: the mosque, shop
or building) and **cups**.

**Is 24,900 too much?** A family can buy a cooler for about 9,000 and pay a
plumber. Ours costs more because it includes a year of upkeep and the proof.
Stage 1 tests exactly this. The fallback is "care and proof only" for a cooler
the family buys itself (the 9,900 plan), which also has the better margin.

**Is 250 a month right for a vendor?** Unknown. It is roughly the profit on a
handful of extra coffees a month. The 20 conversations in stage 1 set the real
number. Vendors are not charged until a district has an audience to show them.

## 7. Three-year model

Produced by `financial_model.py`; change the assumptions there and re-run.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| New sabils sold | 60 | 240 | 600 |
| Company-network coolers | 0 | 40 | 300 |
| New care plans (existing coolers) | 40 | 200 | 500 |
| Renewals (70% renew, *assumption*) | 0 | 70 | 385 |
| Coolers under care at year end | 100 | 550 | 1,785 |
| Paying vendors, average over the year | 10 | 360 | 2,100 |
| Revenue from water services | 1,692,000 | 8,539,000 | 27,826,500 |
| Revenue from vendor plans | 30,000 | 1,080,000 | 6,300,000 |
| **Revenue** | 1,722,000 | 9,619,000 | 34,126,500 |
| Direct costs | 1,278,300 | 6,629,475 | 22,415,662 |
| Gross profit | 443,700 | 2,989,525 | 11,710,838 |
| Gross margin | 25.8% | 31.1% | 34.3% |
| Running costs (team, marketing, tech, legal) | 330,000 | 1,750,000 | 4,250,000 |
| **Profit before tax** | 113,700 | 1,239,525 | 7,460,838 |

**Break-even in year 1:** 58 new sabils cover that year's running costs, with
the founder taking no salary. That is about five sales a month.

**What this model says plainly:**
- Water services bring most of the revenue but at thin margins. They are a
  service business. They pay the bills early and build the map and the trust.
- Vendor plans are small at first but are software income at about 80% margin.
  By year 3 they are under a fifth of revenue and over 40% of gross profit.
  That mix, moving toward software, is the investment case.
- Year 1 only works if the founder is unpaid or has other income.
- Every volume is a target, not a forecast. Stage 1 gives the first real data.

## 9. Legal checklist (to confirm with a lawyer)

| Topic | What I found | Question for the lawyer |
|---|---|---|
| **Collecting donations** | Under the NGO law (149/2019), fundraising needs prior government approval and is done by registered associations; companies are mostly outside that law [9]. | "If my company sells installation and upkeep of a cooler to a customer who chooses to place it for public use, is that a normal sale? Which words must I avoid in adverts (donate, sadaqa shares)? Can several people split one sabil?" |
| **Pooled shares** | Charities sell shares in water projects [7]. For a company this looks like collecting donations. | "Can I offer shares only through a registered charity partner?" |
| **Company form** | A one-person company can be formed by a single founder; recent guides give a minimum capital of EGP 1,000, older ones EGP 50,000 [11]. | "Which form fits, what does it cost in total, and which activities must it list (installation, maintenance, software, advertising)?" |
| **Personal data** | The Personal Data Protection Law (151/2020) got its regulations in November 2025; controllers generally need a licence, with a one-year period to comply that is ending now [21]. The app checks the user's position for the 50 m rule but does not store it. | "Do username accounts, photos and a position that is checked but not stored need a licence? What changes if I add phone numbers?" |
| **Listing vendors** | Mobile food units are licensed under Law 92/2018 with a yearly fee of up to EGP 5,000 [25]; many carts operate without one [24]. | "Can I list a vendor who has no licence? Should paid plans require one?" |
| **Placing coolers in public** | Not researched. | "What permission does a cooler outside a shop or mosque need from the district? Who is liable if someone is harmed by the water?" |
| **Water safety** | Not researched. | "What testing or filter standard should I document to protect the company?" |
| **Store rules** | Google Play requires in-app and web account deletion and a privacy policy [22]. Both are built. | None. |
| **Name** | Not checked. | Trademark search for "Nwasy" and "نواصي" in software and advertising classes. |

Liability for water quality is the risk that could end the company. Before the
first installation: a written host agreement (who pays water and power, who
may switch it off), a filter-change log per cooler shown on its page, and
insurance advice.

## 11. Risks

| Risk | How likely | What limits it |
|---|---|---|
| Families will not pay above the cooler's shop price | Medium. The biggest unknown on the water side. | Stage 1 tests it with real money. Fallback: care-and-proof plan. |
| Vendors will not pay | Medium to high until a district has users. | Free listing first; charge only where usage is proven; the model survives on water alone in year 1. |
| Doing two things at once (water services and vendors) | High for one founder | Strict order: vendors are free and passive until stage 3. |
| Fundraising rules applied to us | Medium | Lawyer first; sell a service; shares only through a charity partner. |
| A cooler makes someone ill | Low, severe | Filter log, host agreement, insurance advice, quick "report a problem". |
| Coolers stolen or vandalised | Medium | Hosts with a doorman or shopkeeper; repairs reserve; cage where needed. |
| The crowd does not confirm | Medium | Start dense in one district; ask patrons' families; vendors confirm their own status daily. |
| Fake confirmations | Low to medium | The 50 m rule, daily caps and rate limits are built; admin review. |
| A big app copies it | Low at first | Density of verified data, plaques and stickers on the street, relationships with hosts and vendors. |
| Thin margin on water | Certain | Wholesale buying, technician routes, renewals, and the growing vendor share. |

## 12. Decisions the founder must make first

1. Side project, or a company you intend to live from? The plan assumes the
   second from stage 2.
2. How many hours a week for the next 3 months?
3. How much money can you risk before the first sale? The plan needs little,
   but not zero.
4. Starting district: Maadi is assumed because the test data is there. Is that
   where you can walk the streets and know people?
5. Do you know a technician or installer you trust?
6. Are you willing to sell in person? Stage 1 is 40 conversations with
   families and 20 with vendors.
